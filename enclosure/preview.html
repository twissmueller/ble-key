How the board is held

Test the snap lips first

The snap lips are the finest detail of both cases: 0.8 mm thick, a 0.25 mm hook, and 0.6 mm gaps beside them. Whether they click well depends on the printer and the filament, so try them on two small test pieces before printing a whole case. Both use the FDM fit (0.35 mm).

ble-key-snap-test-usb.stl
5.8 × 25.0 × 7.8
ble-key-snap-test-battery.stl
5.8 × 25.3 × 14.1
ble-key-snap-test-board.stl
15.0 × 17.8 × 1.25

In out/snap-test-fdm/: a 5.8 mm slice of each base around the board's far supports (floor, walls, supports, lips, end stops) and a stand-in for the board's end. All lie flat on the bed as exported. Regenerate after changing the snap values with enclosure.py --snap-test.

Printing (e.g. Bambu Lab A1 mini, PETG): all three on one plate, 0.2 mm layers — 0.12 mm is better for the lips — 3 walls, no supports. In the slicer preview, check that the gaps beside the lips stay open; PETG strings can bridge them and stiffen the lip.

Testing

  1. Press the board stand-in down onto the supports: it should click in without much force.
  2. Pull it up: it should hold, not pop straight out.
  3. Push a lip outwards and let go: it should spring back, not crack.
  4. Try it with the real XIAO as well; it overhangs the slice, but the edge clicks in the same way.

Tuning in enclosure.py: too loose → raise SNAP_HOOK (0.25 mm); too tight or a lip cracks → lower SNAP_T (0.8 mm) or, in the battery case, deepen the lip's root with SNAP_ROOT (1.6 mm).
